# Solva · Confidence

Every Solva output ships with a confidence number. The number tells
you how much weight to put on the position, not how loud Akki is
willing to be about it.

## What it does

The confidence number reflects the strength of the evidence base
behind the position. It is calibrated against the cited material —
how many independent sources support the claim, how much of the
position rests on weak inference, how clean the contradicting
evidence is.

A high confidence number does not mean Akki is right. It means the
evidence is strong. Decisions remain yours.

## How to use it

1. Read the headline position first.
2. Check the confidence number.
3. If the number is high (say, above 70), spend your reading time on
   the contradicting evidence Akki names — that is where you will
   find the failure mode.
4. If the number is low (say, below 40), spend your reading time on
   what is missing — the position may be early.

**Worked example.** A CFO received a Solva diagnosis with a
confidence of 35 on a recommended channel-mix shift. The low number
prompted the CFO to read the "what is missing" callout rather than
the recommendation. The callout named two data sets the workspace
did not yet hold. The CFO loaded them, re-ran the session, and the
confidence rose to 78 — at which point the position was actionable.

## Common questions

- **Why a number and not a colour?** Because numbers calibrate
  better than traffic lights. A 72 and a 58 are both "amber".
- **Is the scale 0–100?** Yes.
- **Is it comparable across sessions?** Roughly. The calibration is
  consistent within a workspace; cross-workspace comparisons should
  be treated as approximate.

## Troubleshooting

- **The number feels wrong relative to the position.** Open the
  session record and read the bias inventory. Akki may have named a
  bias you did not initially notice.
- **The number is the same on every session.** That is unusual.
  Send the session id to the team so the calibration can be
  inspected.
