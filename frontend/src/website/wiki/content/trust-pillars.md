# Trust · The four pillars

Akki's trust commitments are four pillars. Each pillar is a
principle, not a feature. Features change; pillars do not.

## What it does

The pillars set the terms of engagement between you and the
workspace. They are visible in the Trust Center and surface as
behaviours throughout the product.

## How to use it

1. **Akki refuses to invent.** When Akki cannot cite the evidence, it
   says so plainly. It does not paper over a gap with a confident
   guess. You can rely on this.
2. **Every claim is cited.** Every position Akki returns ships with
   the evidence that supports it. The audit trail is yours.
3. **Decisions stay yours.** Akki is structured to argue, not to
   conclude. The conclusion is your call, in your name, with the
   evidence on the table.
4. **Your data never leaves your account.** Uploads are not training
   material. Anonymisation runs before any reasoning step.

**Worked example.** A pension-fund NED working a sensitive
restructuring question uploaded the board pack and ran a Solva
session. The session diagnosis included the line *"I don't have
evidence for X — recommend pulling the actuarial report before
weighing."* That refusal-to-invent — pillar 1 — was the signal that
prompted the NED to source the missing report rather than act on a
half-grounded recommendation.

## Common questions

- **Which pillar takes priority if they conflict?** They don't, by
  design. The pillars are consistent.
- **Are these audited?** The Trust Center carries the live audit
  trail. Each pillar's evidence is inspectable.
- **What if Akki violates a pillar?** Report it through the feedback
  widget. The session and the violation get attention from the
  team.

## Troubleshooting

- **A Solva session returned a position without citations.** This is
  a pillar violation. Save the session id, hit the feedback widget,
  and the team will investigate.
- **The pillars don't appear in the Trust Center.** Refresh. If the
  pillars are still absent the tile is failing — see /status.
