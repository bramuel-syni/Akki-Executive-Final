Akki Website Brief v3
Reanchored on safety and governance. Aligned to the Founding Cohort v3 voice.
This brief replaces Website Brief v2. It is a full rewrite — not a patch — because the conversion thesis has changed. Where v2 anchored on Akki as the executive thinking system with Solva's reasoning architecture as the differentiator, v3 anchors on safety and governance: Akki as the AI environment that lets executives use AI fully on real institutional material without the governance exposure that consumer LLMs create.
The new anchor is more compelling because it speaks to a real, immediate, unspoken pain — senior people are already using AI in ways their organisations have not formally sanctioned, and they know it. v3 names that reality directly and positions Akki as the architectural answer. The voice and lexicon throughout this brief match the Founding Cohort Deck v3, so cohort recipients arriving on the website encounter the same language they read in the deck.

## 1.5 Tone calibration
Every page on the website should pass three tone tests:
1.  The Economist test. Could the headline appear in The Economist's print edition without modification? If yes, the tone is right. If it sounds like a SaaS landing page, it's wrong.
2.  The senior peer test. Would a senior person we respect — a former bank CEO, a sitting Cabinet Secretary, a NED with three boards — find the page condescending, sycophantic, or patronising? If yes, rewrite. If they would read it without flinching, the tone is right.
3.  The restraint test. If you removed every adjective and adverb, would the meaning survive? If yes, the page is restrained. If the meaning collapses without the modifiers, the page is over-reaching.

## 4.2 Section: "What's actually happening"
Reproduces the three patterns from the homepage Movement 1 with more depth. Each pattern gets its own subsection — pattern, why it happens, what the exposure is.
### Pattern 1 — Board packs into consumer LLMs
Body copy:
Pattern 1 body
Board packs are 200-400 pages. The cycle is short. The chairman expects pre-reads to be more than skimmed. The senior person who can summarise efficiently has hours back; the one who cannot is up until midnight. Most senior people we know now use ChatGPT or Claude to compress board packs into something they can hold in their head before the meeting.
What the exposure is
Board pack content frequently includes: customer names with concentration disclosure, financial detail not yet public, strategic positions ahead of board endorsement, and personnel matters with privacy implications. Pasting this into a consumer LLM means the data leaves your jurisdiction, lands on infrastructure not subject to your governance framework, and may be retained or used for model training depending on terms most users have not read.

### Pattern 2 — Sensitive correspondence in chat
Body copy:
Pattern 2 body
Letters to regulators that need to defend a position. Difficult emails to reportees that need to land without escalating. Position papers for the audit committee, the cabinet, the executive committee. The senior person drafts the first version in ChatGPT or Gemini because the alternative is staring at a blank page for an hour.
What the exposure is
Correspondence drafts often include: counterparty names, regulatory matter references, internal personnel context, and legal-strategic positioning. The draft contains real names and real situations even if you delete them later. Once the draft has touched consumer infrastructure, the exposure exists regardless of what the final sent version looks like.

### Pattern 3 — Strategic decisions through AI
Body copy:
Pattern 3 body
Should I push back on the Chair next Tuesday? How do I sequence the conversation with the CFO who has lost confidence in the FD? The genuine strategic decisions of senior life — where the textbook answer is wrong because the people involved are real — are the questions executives now think through with AI as a sounding board. The AI is genuinely helpful. It is also reading and processing material your governance framework would not knowingly authorise.
What the exposure is
These conversations include real names, real organisational context, and real strategic intent. They are precisely the conversations that — if disclosed in a future deposition, regulatory request, or media inquiry — would be most damaging. The exposure is not just about data; it's about position-formation that becomes discoverable.

## 5.2 Section: The three architectural commitments
Each commitment gets its own subsection. The pattern: claim, mechanism, what it means in practice, link to the deeper Trust & Sovereignty page.
### Commitment 1 — De-identification
Claim
The LLM never knows who you are.
Body:
Body
Synisense Shield is the layer between Akki and the language model. Before any LLM sees a conversation — even Akki's own internal calls — names, identifiers, account numbers, case references, and other re-identifying tokens are replaced. Re-identification happens only on the way back to you. The LLM works on de-identified content; you work on the real thing.

In practice
When you upload a board pack mentioning your chairman by name and your bank's actual financial figures, the LLM sees "Director_47," "Institution_3," and "Figure_12." Akki maps these back to your reality before showing you anything.

### Commitment 2 — Encryption at rest
Claim
Even Syni.ai engineers cannot read your data.
Body:
Body
Material you upload to Akki is encrypted with keys derived from your authentication. The encrypted data sits in storage; the keys do not sit with us. Without the keys, the data is opaque — to attackers, to subpoena, to our own engineering team. Privacy is a property of the architecture, not a policy promise.

In practice — what this means
If Syni.ai is compromised, your institutional data is not exposed. If a court orders us to produce your data, we cannot — we do not have the keys. If our engineering team has a curiosity moment, the architecture stops them, not policy. If you leave Akki, your data leaves with you — encrypted, exportable, and ours to delete.

### Commitment 3 — Refusal and provenance
Claim
If evidence is thin, Akki tells you. If a claim is unsourced, Akki refuses.
Body:
Body
Two refusal mechanisms work in different parts of Akki. Solva refuses to weight scenarios when the evidence in front of it cannot support a probability-weighted analysis — it names what's missing, gives you the candidate framings without weighting, and tells you what would change the picture. Work Studio refuses to incorporate claims into drafts that cannot be sourced from your attached materials — "this claim isn't sourced from your materials. We can't add it without a citation."

In practice
The system that says no is the system safe to give institutional material to. Refusal protects you from the most dangerous failure mode of consumer LLMs — confident output where confidence isn't warranted.

SECTION 10
# CTAs and conversion architecture
v3 has a different CTA architecture from v2 because the conversion thesis is different. v2's primary CTAs were "Talk to us" and "Read more." v3's primary CTAs convert to one of three outcomes — sandbox, cohort, or pilot.
## 10.1 The three conversion outcomes
1.  Try the sandbox. The fastest, lowest-friction conversion. Visitor experiences Akki for eight minutes and leaves with a downloadable artefact. Most homepage and product page CTAs route here.
2.  Be one of the first twenty (Founding Cohort). Higher-commitment conversion. Visitor enters the cohort flow — confirmation call, onboarding, play, feedback. Most depth-content page CTAs route here.
3.  Talk to us about a pilot (Government). Specific to government and regulator visitors. Visitor enters a pilot conversation. For Government page primary CTA; appears as a secondary CTA on Trust & Sovereignty.

## 10.3 CTA copy rules
•  Primary CTAs are imperative and specific. "Try the sandbox" not "Learn more." "Be one of the first twenty" not "Join us."
•  Never use "Get started" — too generic, too vendor-cadence
•  Never use "Sign up" — Akki is something you do, not something you sign up for
•  Never use "Request a demo" — the sandbox is the demo, and the cohort is the deeper conversation
•  CTAs that route to email use the executive's name where possible: "Reply to this email →" rather than "Contact us"
•  Calendar CTAs specify duration: "Book a 20-minute confirmation call" not "Book a meeting"

## 12.2 Quality assurance
